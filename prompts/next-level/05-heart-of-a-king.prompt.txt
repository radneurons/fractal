Create a 25.00-second cartographic typography film titled "THE HEART OF A KING."

Elizabeth I tells her army that her body may be weak but her resolve is not. Make that change of strength happen inside the letters themselves. Every word uses one variable font, and its weight axis carries the story:
- hairline for "weak and feeble"
- black for "the heart and stomach of a king"
The same glyphs thicken continuously. They are never swapped for a bolder copy.

The world is a hand-engraved 16th-century sea chart:
- The coastline of England is typeset from the speech itself.
- The enemy fleet is made of words.
- The border of the realm is a line of type that holds.

4. SIGNATURE TECHNIQUES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
A. WEIGHT AS RESOLVE. One continuous axis animation drives the central transformation. Make wght and opsz functions of t, applied per glyph with a stagger.

B. TYPE-ON-PATH COASTLINE. England's southern and eastern coast is drawn as a smooth Bézier path. The words "and of a king of England too" are typeset along it:
- Each glyph is placed by arc length and rotated to the tangent.
- Keep kerning-aware spacing along the curve.
- The coastline line itself is the baseline.

C. WORD-SHIPS. PARMA, SPAIN, and PRINCE OF EUROPE are each set as the hull of a ship:
- The word is the hull, in solid ink.
- Above it rise engraved masts and hatched sails, generated procedurally from the word's width so the sails scale to the hull.
- Each ship rides the hatched sea with a gentle closed-form pitch and roll.

D. THE BORDER HOLDS. "the borders of my realm" is a vermilion line of type that runs along the coast, parallel to and just offshore of the coastline type. When the fleet reaches it, the border does not move. Instead its weight axis rises from 300 to 900 in place, and the ships stop against it, turn about, and recede.
